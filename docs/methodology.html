Axiovo data coverage and methodology

Axiovo is an independent, experimental open-source research workspace, not an official arXiv or SciRate service. This page documents what the site actually does and what it cannot guarantee.

Cryptology ePrint: official records and limitations

Axiovo indexes metadata from the IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive using its OAI-PMH metadata service. The ePrint paper identifier is year/number, for example 2026/1234; it is not an arXiv identifier and cannot be converted to one. All record links point to the original archive.

Only the metadata available in the harvested windows is searchable locally. Historical years are backfilled progressively and the count of returned records is not the full archive count. OAI-PMH datestamps record metadata updates, not independently verified first-submission dates. We do not invent exact revision histories or publication citations when they are not supplied.

Selected topical labels such as quantum cryptography or post-quantum cryptography are transparent Axiovo search refinements, not claims about an official IACR category taxonomy. No paper PDFs are redistributed. For comprehensive historic results, search the official archive directly. Saved papers remain private in the browser and are backed up only when you explicitly export them.

Research Desk and archive directories

Each archive explorer uses the archive’s published subjects as a starting point. Axiovo adds its own research subfields and sub-subtopics with search terms and aliases. Those layers are editable on this device and can be shared as links. The Research Desk searches across archives and separates results and coverage by source. arXiv categories follow its official taxonomy. The bioRxiv directory includes its historical Clinical Trials and Epidemiology subjects. The ChemRxiv directory includes the published subject labels used across its platforms.

arXiv date ranges use verified UTC first-submission batches wherever those batches exist. Other arXiv results come from available snapshots and arXiv-registered DOI metadata via DataCite. Search by an exact revision to find a matching cached record or open the pinned arXiv source directly.

bioRxiv subject searches use its official API. BioRxiv keyword, author and sub-subtopic searches use Europe PMC preprint metadata and verify the publisher. When a record has no native subject tag, the match is based on its title or abstract and marked as such. ChemRxiv uses Crossref DOI records. Chemistry subject searches use keywords where the metadata omits subject tags. These provider results and snapshots have partial historical coverage.

Shortlists, search presets and reading notes are stored in your browser. Search terms are sent to the selected metadata providers when you run a search. Abstract playback uses installed English voices where available. The homepage tour has a separate prerecorded narration.

Discover: two source scopes, one research focus

Latest arXiv uses a periodically generated local set of selected arXiv categories and curated historical IDs. Its index count is not the entire arXiv archive. Explore my field uses the public OpenAlex API to search works with an arXiv-hosted location and checks discipline/topic relevance. It can search far beyond the local snapshot but its results depend on OpenAlex coverage, source metadata, rate limits and classification. Missing papers in Axiovo are not evidence of absence from arXiv.

A single researcher focus controls main field, area and subtopic; starred interests are saved in the browser. The official arXiv search remains available for independent cross-checking.

Statistical mechanics and condensed matter

Statistical mechanics and condensed matter physics have separate curated main fields, each with 12 research areas and 48 related search interests. These are navigation and query aids, not independently validated subject classifications. The broader OpenAlex search screens arXiv-hosted literature using field metadata and relevant terms. The limited local snapshot may contain relatively few papers from cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, cond-mat.supr-con, and other condensed-matter categories. The arXiv categories are not exhaustively harvested here. For authoritative coverage, use arXiv's condensed matter archive.

High-energy physics and AI limitations

High-energy physics has a dedicated main field covering particle phenomenology, QFT, collider experiments, lattice gauge theory, supersymmetry, strings, EFTs, scattering amplitudes and particle cosmology. ArXiv uses separate hep-th, hep-ph, hep-ex and hep-lat archives; the local feed is not exhaustive. Wider searches are metadata-restricted and depend on OpenAlex coverage.

The Assistant can detect models running on a user's Ollama service, and offers structured technical review and derivation-assumption audit modes. AI-generated citations, technical claims and equation interpretations remain unverified unless the original papers and mathematical calculations are independently examined. The public website does not run a hosted model.

Cross-listing, bioRxiv and external research evidence

ArXiv cross-list badges are derived from the Atom API's category list relative to its primary category. The *Cross-listed only* filter requires at least one secondary category. Cross-listing is not an AI topic prediction.

bioRxiv is a separate life-science preprint archive. Axiovo uses a periodically refreshed recent metadata snapshot from its public API and identifies records by DOI. The snapshot does not cover all historical bioRxiv manuscripts, and a preprint should not be treated as peer-reviewed merely because it appears here. Saved bioRxiv records and notes remain in browser storage.

Consensus search is optional. The locally run Axiovo server can forward a researcher query using a private `CONSENSUS_API_KEY` environment variable, never included in public JavaScript. GitHub Pages does not include this proxy or any server-held API key. Consensus uses its own API allowance and may require user registration. On the hosted website, follow its official search link instead.

Paper identifiers and version fidelity

An unversioned arXiv identifier links to the latest available revision on arxiv.org. A suffix such as v1 or v2 requests that exact revision, if it exists. OpenAlex's submitted/accepted/published manuscript classifications do not identify arXiv revision numbers. Publication dates in OpenAlex are not guaranteed to match the first arXiv submission date. Confirm priority, revisions and source PDFs on the original arXiv record.

Scientific relevance and AI

Field Explorer filters OpenAlex results by research field and textual topic evidence. If keyword results are sparse, a user-controlled OpenAlex embedding-based semantic search may suggest additional arXiv-hosted candidates, which are screened again. Semantic matching improves retrieval but does not establish that a candidate is relevant, novel, correct or equivalent to another theorem.

Research Assistant supports deterministic, source-linked abstract-level evidence and optional locally executed Qwen models (Ollama or experimental browser WebGPU). No hosted Axiovo general-purpose language model is activated. Model-generated interpretations and citations must be checked against the full original papers. Research Tools supports local PDF viewing and notes, comparisons, citation exploration and manual research update checks.

Library, profiles and collaboration

Saved arXiv IDs, specific revisions, paper notes, field interests, research questions and local researcher profiles live in browser storage unless the user separately configures an optional account/sync service. Clearing browser data can erase them. Export important reading and research data. Public comments and reactions currently rely on GitHub Issues; dedicated Axiovo social accounts depend on separately configured Supabase integration.

Updates and limitations

GitHub Actions rebuilds selected arXiv feeds and site assets on scheduled runs. These jobs may be delayed, fail or be paused. The daily OAI-PMH process adds a bounded subset of arXiv metadata, not a full historical mirror. Axiovo is a static GitHub Pages application and has no continuously running ingestion server. Full arXiv recall, authenticated community features and centrally synchronized research workspaces would require additional infrastructure.
